# **Guide to Writing the "Proposed Method / Solution" Section**

## **Target Grade: Excellent (\>80%)**

**Mapped Criterion:** Apply Systems (10%)

This guide outlines how to structure your "Proposed Method / Solution" section to specifically hit the *Excellent* criteria from the ENG40011 rubric.

### **1\. Unified Architecture & Integration**

**Rubric Requirement:** *Clear integration of all subsystems into a unified architecture.*

* **What to write:** Do not just list components (e.g., "We used an Arduino and a sensor"). Instead, explain **how** they talk to each other to form a complete system.  
* **Structure:**  
  * **System Overview:** Start with a high-level paragraph describing the entire solution's workflow (Input $\\to$ Process $\\to$ Output).  
  * **Subsystem Breakdown:** Divide your solution into logical subsystems (e.g., *Power Subsystem*, *Control Subsystem*, *Mechanical Subsystem*).  
  * **Interface Description:** Explicitly state how Subsystem A connects to Subsystem B (e.g., "The microcontroller triggers the relay via a 5V logic signal, which then activates the 240V pump...").

### **2\. Visuals & Diagrams**

**Rubric Requirement:** *Detailed and accurate diagrams/schematics.*

* **What to include:** High-quality, professional figures. Avoid blurry screenshots or hand-drawn sketches unless they are polished.  
* **Required Diagrams:**  
  1. **System Block Diagram:** A high-level view showing all subsystems and flow of data/energy between them.  
  2. **Flowcharts:** For software or logic, show the decision-making process.  
* *Tip: Label every figure (Figure 1, Figure 2\) and refer to them in the text (e.g., "As shown in Figure 1...").*

### **3\. Calculations & Justification**

**Rubric Requirement:** *All calculations correct, justified, and clearly explained.*

* **What to write:** This is the "Why" behind your design. You must justify why you chose specific components over others using math/physics.  
* **Examples:**  
  * **Structural:** Stress/strain calculations to justify material choice.  
  * **Electrical:** Power budget calculations to justify battery size (e.g., $Capacity \= Current \\times Time$).  
  * **Mechanical:** Gear ratio or torque calculations to justify motor selection.  
* *Formatting:* Use Equation Editor for clear mathematical presentation. Number your equations.

### **4\. Connection to User Needs**

**Rubric Requirement:** *Design decisions directly tied to end-user needs and constraints.*

* **What to write:** explicitly link back to the **Need Analysis** you submitted in Week 3\.  
* **Strategy:**  
  * "We selected the XYZ sensor because the Need Analysis identified a requirement for \<Specific Accuracy\>..."  
  * "The device enclosure is IP67 rated to address the user constraint of outdoor operation in rainy conditions."  
  * Create a **Traceability Matrix** (optional but impressive): A table mapping *User Requirement ID* $\\to$ *Design Feature* $\\to$ *Verification Method*.

### **5\. Scalability & Contextual Factors**

**Rubric Requirement:** *Environmental, regulatory, or scalability considerations are addressed.*

* **What to write:** Discuss the broader impact and future of the design.  
* **Key Points to Cover:**  
  * **Scalability:** If you built a small prototype, how would this work if mass-produced? (e.g., "Moving from 3D printing to Injection Molding").  
  * **Regulations/Standards:** Mention relevant standards (e.g., "Designed in compliance with AS/NZS 3000 for wiring" or "ISO 13485 for medical devices").  
  * **Sustainability:** Materials used, power efficiency, or end-of-life recycling.

### **Checklist for "Excellent"**

* \[ \] Is the System Block Diagram included and clearly labeled?  
* \[ \] Have I shown the math (calculations) for selecting key components (Battery, Motor, Beams)?  
* \[ \] Did I explicitly mention the "User Needs" or "Need Analysis" constraints?  
* \[ \] Is there a section discussing standards (ISO/AS/ASTM) or safety regulations?  
* \[ \] Is the text professional (third person, technical language)?
